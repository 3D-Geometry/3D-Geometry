Abstract

3D object pose estimation, including rotation and translation, is a fundamental problem in computer vision and robotics. However, visual ambiguity, occlusion, and geometric symmetry often make a single visual observation compatible with multiple plausible poses. Conventional deterministic approaches struggle to represent this inherent uncertainty. In this work, we present a probabilistic framework based on conditional score-based diffusion models for 3D object pose estimation. Rather than predicting a single pose, our approach models a distribution over candidate rotations. To preserve the geometric structure of the rotation space, the forward diffusion process is defined directly on the Special Orthogonal Group SO(3), using an isotropic Gaussian distribution. Visual features extracted by a ResNet-34 encoder and continuous matrix representations of rotation are incorporated into a conditional score network through cross-attention mechanisms and time embeddings. During inference, reverse-time sampling is performed using Euler–Maruyama discretization, with rotation updates implemented through group multiplication on the manifold. The proposed framework enables multimodal rotation estimation and explicit representation of pose uncertainty. Its performance is evaluated using geometric metrics, including geodesic error and threshold-based accuracy.

Key Ideas

The main components of our probabilistic pose estimation framework.

01. Score-Based Diffusion

A conditional score network learns the underlying distribution of plausible object rotations instead of producing a single deterministic prediction.

02. Geometric Consistency

Diffusion is performed directly on SO(3), respecting the geometric structure of the rotation manifold throughout the sampling process.

03. Pose Uncertainty

Multiple reverse diffusion trajectories allow the framework to represent multimodal rotation distributions and quantify uncertainty.
